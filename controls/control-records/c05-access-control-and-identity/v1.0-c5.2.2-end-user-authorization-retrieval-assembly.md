# End-user Authorization Across Retrieval and Assembly

AISVS Verification Level: 2

## Upstream basis

This Control interprets OWASP AISVS `v1.0-C5.2.2`, a Level 2 Requirement in
C5.2, AI Resource Authorization & Classification. The normative chapter and the
corresponding Research page were inspected at the immutable revision recorded in
the front matter. The Research page was marked "Last Researched: 2026-07-14" in
that snapshot.

The normative Requirement calls for the end user's authorization context to be
enforced at each retrieval and assembly stage instead of relying only on the
service account's permissions. It names RAG queries and embedding lookups as
examples, but the assurance property applies to equivalent retrieval pipelines.

The Research page supplies useful examples such as candidate retrieval,
reranking, parent-chunk expansion, prompt assembly, citations, and response caches.
Those examples help identify stages where authorization can be lost; they do not
mandate a particular vendor, protocol, Policy engine, or number of authorization
service calls.

## Interpretation

Every data-bearing retrieval and assembly stage must restrict its output to data
that the initiating end user is authorized to access for the current request.

The pipeline may use a broadly privileged workload or service identity to perform
technical operations. That identity must not determine which data the end user may
retrieve, place into Model context, cite, or receive. The effective data set must
instead be constrained by trusted authorization context bound to the end user and
request.

"At each stage" describes an end-to-end result, not a required topology. A stage
does not have to contact a remote Policy Decision Point for every function call if
the architecture can demonstrate that:

- the authorized candidate set was derived from trusted, sufficiently current
  authorization data;
- no downstream stage can add data outside that set;
- all alternate, fallback, cache, expansion, and error paths preserve the same
  restriction; and
- missing or unverifiable authorization context cannot turn into an unrestricted
  query or assembly operation.

An opaque identifier may be discovered before object-level authorization when the
identifier itself is not sensitive and no protected content or metadata is
materialized outside the authorization boundary. The assessor must identify that
boundary explicitly. Retrieving protected chunks broadly and filtering only after
they have entered an untrusted Pipeline or Model context does not satisfy this
Control.

Authorization context must come from a trusted identity and Policy path. A tenant,
role, group, object identifier, or filter supplied only in user text, Model output,
Agent reasoning, or another caller-editable field is not trusted authorization
context.

## Security objective

Prevent a retrieval service from acting as a confused deputy that uses its own
broad access to obtain or assemble data the initiating end user could not access
directly.

In practical terms: relevance decides which authorized data is useful; it must not
decide which data is authorized.

The Control also keeps unauthorized content out of Model context. This reduces the
chance that the Model can disclose the content through summarization, inference,
citation, encoding, or a later Tool call.

## Applicability

This Control applies when an end user initiates a Pipeline that retrieves or
assembles data with access rules that differ between users, groups, roles, tenants,
purposes, or other authorization attributes. Examples include:

- RAG over enterprise or customer documents;
- semantic, vector, keyword, graph, SQL, or hybrid retrieval used to construct
  Model context;
- retrieval followed by reranking, parent-document expansion, or multi-source
  aggregation;
- Agent or Tool flows that retrieve records on behalf of a user;
- cached retrieval results, assembled prompts, citations, or responses reused
  across requests; and
- pipelines in which a service account can access more data than the end user.

The Control applies whether the underlying resources are in one store or many and
whether authorization is enforced by the source system, retrieval service, trusted
Policy layer, or a combination of them.

### Non-applicability

The Control may be not applicable when:

- the assessed system performs no retrieval or data assembly; or
- every item available to the Pipeline is intentionally public to every possible
  end user, and no user-, group-, role-, tenant-, or purpose-specific access rule
  exists.

Non-applicability must be supported by the actual data and access model. A label
such as "internal," "shared knowledge base," or "read-only" is not sufficient.
C5.2.1 may still require access control over the AI Resource itself even when all
content has the same audience.

## Scope and assumptions

The assessment must identify:

- the initiating end-user principal and how it is authenticated;
- the service or workload identities used by each Pipeline component;
- the authoritative source of the user's entitlements;
- the authorization-relevant attributes, such as tenant, group, role, purpose,
  classification, resource, and action;
- every stage that discovers, retrieves, filters, reranks, expands, combines,
  cites, caches, or restores data;
- the point at which protected content and metadata are materialized;
- the Policy Decision and Policy Enforcement Points used at each relevant boundary;
- fallback, retry, partial-failure, and degraded-mode behavior;
- cache keys, invalidation behavior, and the maximum accepted authorization-staleness
  window; and
- any stage at which Model- or Agent-generated values influence a retrieval query.

This Control assumes that an authoritative identity and entitlement source exists.
It can test whether the Pipeline enforces that source, but it cannot independently
prove that upstream identities, group membership, classification, or Policy are
correct.

The phrase "each retrieval and assembly stage" is not exhaustively defined by
AISVS. This record interprets it as every stage capable of introducing protected
data or authorization-relevant metadata into a candidate set, Model context,
citation, cached result, or user-visible response.

## Assets, actors, identities, and trust boundaries

### Assets

- source documents, records, fields, chunks, embeddings, and associated metadata;
- the user's current entitlements and authorization attributes;
- candidate sets, reranked sets, expanded documents, assembled prompts, citations,
  caches, and responses; and
- Policy decisions and evidence showing how a candidate was allowed or denied.

### Actors and identities

- the initiating end user;
- an attacker controlling user input or using a legitimately authenticated but
  less-privileged account;
- the application and retrieval Pipeline workload identities;
- the identity provider and authoritative entitlement source;
- Policy Decision and Enforcement Points;
- retrieval, reranking, assembly, cache, Model, Agent, and Tool components; and
- operators or services that can change routing, filters, Policy, or cache scope.

### Trust boundaries

1. **End user to application:** untrusted request content crosses into the
   application; trusted identity context must be established separately.
2. **Application to authorization path:** the user's identity and request context
   are resolved into an authorization decision or constrained data scope.
3. **Pipeline to source or index:** a broadly privileged service identity may cross
   this boundary, but end-user entitlement must still constrain the operation.
4. **Between Pipeline stages:** candidate data moves through filtering, reranking,
   expansion, aggregation, and assembly without gaining unauthorized items.
5. **Pipeline to Model or Agent:** only the authorized assembled context may cross
   into a probabilistic or otherwise untrusted component.
6. **Cache to later request:** cached data must not cross to a principal or Policy
   state for which it was not authorized.

## Required security properties

### SP-1: Trusted end-user context

Authorization decisions use identity and entitlement context established through a
trusted channel and bound to the current request. User content, Model output, or
Agent-generated attributes cannot replace or broaden that context.

### SP-2: Authorized data set at every stage

The data-bearing output of each retrieval and assembly stage is a subset of the
data authorized for the initiating end user. Reranking, expansion, aggregation,
citation generation, and equivalent later stages cannot reintroduce excluded data.

### SP-3: Service authority is not user authority

A service or workload identity's ability to access an item is insufficient to
include that item. Inclusion also requires a permit for the end-user principal and
the relevant resource, action, and request context.

### SP-4: Fail closed without usable authorization

Missing, invalid, expired, unverifiable, or unavailable authorization context does
not produce an unrestricted retrieval or assembly operation. The safe result is
denial, an empty authorized result, or another explicitly safe failure.

### SP-5: Authorization scope survives reuse and alternate paths

Caches, retries, fallbacks, secondary retrievers, parent-document expansion, and
other alternate paths preserve the same or a narrower authorization scope. Cached
artifacts are bound to all attributes whose change could alter the permit decision,
or are re-authorized before reuse.

### SP-6: Authorization freshness is explicit and testable

Entitlement changes and revocations take effect within a documented maximum window
appropriate to the protected data. Index or decision-cache delay cannot silently
create an unlimited period in which revoked content remains retrievable.

## Scope calibration and adjacent assurance

| Observed condition | C5.2.2 result | Separate assurance impact |
|---|---|---|
| A broad service account performs the query, but trusted end-user entitlement deterministically constrains every data-bearing stage | Pass, if bypass, cache, and failure paths preserve that constraint | Service credential scope and isolation still require separate review |
| The initial vector search is user-filtered, but parent-document expansion fetches the full unauthorized parent | Fail | The later assembly stage lost the user's authorization scope |
| The Pipeline retrieves protected chunks using only the service account and relies on the Model or a final DLP filter not to disclose them | Fail | C5.2.4 output filtering may add defense in depth but does not repair C5.2.2 |
| A trusted component discovers opaque, non-sensitive candidate IDs and authorizes them before protected content or metadata is materialized | Potential pass | The materialization boundary and sensitivity of identifiers must be demonstrated |
| Tenant or group filters are copied from the user's prompt or Model-generated query | Fail | Input validation alone cannot make caller-controlled authorization context trusted |
| A response cache is keyed only by query text and returns another user's authorized result | Fail | Cache isolation and invalidation may also overlap C5.3 or C8 |
| Dedicated per-user storage and trusted routing prevent any Pipeline stage from addressing another user's data | Pass, if the routing and all alternate paths are verified | Physical or tenant isolation strength is assessed separately |
| Retrieval authorization is correct, but shared Model infrastructure leaks another tenant's cache through a side channel | Pass for C5.2.2 only | Evaluate C5.3.1 and C5.3.2; the overall system remains insecure |
| Retrieval and assembly are correct, but the Model reveals unauthorized data already present through another context source | Pass for C5.2.2 only | Evaluate context provenance and C5.2.4 output entitlement enforcement |

## Threat and failure-mode rationale

The primary failure is confused-deputy behavior. The Pipeline has authority to read
many records and performs an operation for a less-privileged user without preserving
that user's restrictions.

Representative attacker capabilities and failures include:

- an authenticated user submits a query likely to match another group or tenant's
  documents;
- a prompt injection asks an Agent, retriever, or Tool to omit or replace a tenant,
  group, or object filter;
- a Model generates a resource identifier or filter that is treated as trusted
  authorization context;
- a reranker, secondary retriever, parent expansion, citation resolver, or fallback
  path uses only the service identity;
- a revoked grant remains effective through stale index metadata or authorization
  caches; and
- a query-only cache returns content assembled for a different principal or Policy
  state.

The impacts include cross-user or cross-tenant disclosure, unauthorized placement
of data into Model context, and subsequent propagation through responses, citations,
logs, memory, Tools, or caches.

No MITRE ATLAS technique mapping is asserted in this revision. Prompt Injection can
trigger or steer the failure, but it does not define the missing authorization
property. A broad Prompt Injection mapping would add context without precisely
representing the confused-deputy and stage-by-stage authorization failure.

## Verification

### Architecture and configuration review

1. Draw the end-to-end data flow from end user through authentication,
   authorization, retrieval, reranking, expansion, assembly, Model context,
   citation, cache, and response.
2. Record the effective identity used at every hop and distinguish end-user
   identity from service or workload identity.
3. Identify every stage capable of introducing protected content or metadata.
4. For each stage, identify the trusted authorization inputs, Policy authority,
   Enforcement Point, failure behavior, and output data set.
5. Inspect how entitlement and classification changes reach indexes, Policy caches,
   response caches, and long-running sessions.
6. Trace all fallback, retry, cache-hit, direct-source, Tool, and administrative
   paths that can produce assembled context.

The review fails when a relevant stage, identity, data source, or alternate path
cannot be enumerated well enough to verify the end-user restriction.

### Positive verification

Use at least two principals with deliberately different access:

- User A can access document set A but not set B.
- User B can access document set B but not set A.
- The service identity can access both sets.

Run the same semantically equivalent query as both users. Verify that candidate
sets, reranked and expanded results, assembled context, citations, cache entries,
and responses contain only the requesting user's authorized subset. Verify that an
explicitly shared document can be returned to both users without weakening the
disjoint restrictions.

### Negative and abuse-case verification

| ID | Test | Expected result |
|---|---|---|
| N-1 | As User A, query for distinctive content that exists only in User B's document while the service identity can read both | No B content or sensitive metadata appears in any data-bearing intermediate stage, Model context, citation, cache, or response |
| N-2 | Put a different tenant, group, role, object ID, or filter in user text or Model-generated retrieval arguments | The trusted authorization context remains unchanged and the unauthorized item is denied or excluded |
| N-3 | Remove, corrupt, expire, or make the end-user authorization context unverifiable | The Pipeline denies or returns an empty authorized result; it never falls back to service-account-only retrieval |
| N-4 | Force reranker, parent-document expansion, secondary retriever, retry, or degraded mode after an initially authorized result | Every later data-bearing result remains within the original or a narrower authorized set |
| N-5 | Prime a cache as User B, then issue the same query as User A or after a Policy change | The cached artifact is not reused unless it is valid for the new principal and current authorization-relevant context |
| N-6 | Revoke User A's grant at the authoritative source, then repeat retrieval before and after the declared propagation window | Access stops within the documented maximum window; indefinite stale authorization fails |
| N-7 | Make the authorization service or entitlement source unavailable | The protected retrieval path fails closed and does not issue an unfiltered query |
| N-8 | Request an authorized child chunk whose parent contains unauthorized sections | Expansion returns only authorized content or is denied; the full unauthorized parent is not assembled |

Tests must inspect intermediate Pipeline artifacts in a safe test environment where
practical. Checking only the final natural-language response can miss unauthorized
content that entered Model context but happened not to be repeated.

### Failure conditions

The Control fails when any of the following is observed:

- a service-account permit is accepted as sufficient end-user authorization;
- caller- or Model-controlled data can broaden trusted authorization context;
- any retrieval or assembly stage introduces protected data outside the user's
  authorized set;
- a cache, fallback, retry, expansion, citation, or Tool path bypasses the user
  restriction;
- unavailable or unusable authorization context produces an unrestricted query;
- revoked access remains usable beyond the documented and accepted propagation
  window; or
- the assessor cannot determine which principal, Policy, and Enforcement Point
  governed a data-bearing stage.

The following are important but do not independently pass this Control:

- a content-safety or DLP filter on the final response;
- a system prompt instructing the Model not to reveal private data;
- a service account with access to the complete corpus;
- a tenant or role string present only in the prompt;
- a metadata-filter configuration without end-to-end negative tests; or
- a diagram showing an authorization component without evidence that all relevant
  paths use it.

## Known limitations and uncertainty

- Passing this Control does not prove that the upstream identity, entitlement,
  classification, or Policy data is correct.
- The Requirement does not prescribe an acceptable revocation or cache-staleness
  interval. The assessor must judge whether the documented maximum is appropriate
  to the data and threat model.
- Metadata filters can be a valid Enforcement Point, but their correctness depends
  on metadata integrity, query construction, store semantics, and complete coverage
  of all paths.
- The exact boundary between discovery of opaque candidate IDs and retrieval of
  protected data is architecture-dependent and must be stated explicitly.
- Authorization-aware retrieval does not prevent Prompt Injection, poisoning,
  Model memorization, unsafe Tool use, or inference from legitimately authorized
  data.
- It does not guarantee post-inference entitlement filtering, classification-label
  propagation, or infrastructure-level tenant isolation; adjacent Requirements
  cover those properties.
- Negative tests can demonstrate known authorization boundaries but cannot prove
  the absence of every undocumented retrieval or assembly path.

No Engineering Pattern Mapping has been assessed. A future Pattern may address one
or more Enforcement Points without fully satisfying the end-to-end Control.
